Booking widget

A themeable, accessible scheduling widget for gyms, salons and consultants: pick a service, a person and a time, and confirm in under a minute.

Independent concept project. The studio is fictional. Source code available on request.

The brief

Small businesses that take appointments usually embed a third-party scheduler that looks nothing like their site. I set myself the brief of a booking flow that takes on the site’s colours and type, works on a phone, and is pleasant with only a keyboard.

What I built

  • Four steps: service, coach (or “any coach”), date and time, details, with a progress bar and clickable breadcrumbs
  • A month calendar that marks open days and loads with a skeleton, and times grouped into morning, afternoon and evening
  • Times shown in the visitor’s own time zone, switchable, with a 24h and 12h clock
  • After booking: an .ics file for Apple and Outlook, a Google Calendar link, reschedule and cancel
  • A demo page with a live customiser for colour, corners, font, light or dark, and preview width

Four decisions

Time zones without a library. Slots are worked out in the studio’s zone and shown in the visitor’s, using only the browser’s Intl API. Days are grouped by the visitor’s calendar, and the hour that is skipped when clocks go forward is never offered.

No double bookings. The time is checked again at the moment you confirm. If someone was faster, you go back to the times still free, with a short note instead of an error page.

It feels live. Booking in one tab removes the time from every other open tab. A “simulate other visitors” switch shows a taken time fading out while you look at it.

It fits any site. The widget has its own scoped styles and four theme variables. Button text switches between dark and light for contrast, and the layout follows the widget’s own width with container queries, so it works in a sidebar as well as full width.

Accessibility

The calendar follows the grid pattern: arrow keys move by day or week, Home and End by week, Page Up and Page Down by month, and Enter picks. Each day announces how many free times it has, focus moves to the heading of every new step, and form errors are tied to their fields. Motion is reduced when the system asks for it.

Testing

18 unit tests cover time zone conversion including the change to and from summer time, free slots, notice periods and breaks, double booking, cancel and reschedule, the calendar file and colour contrast. I also checked the full flow in a browser at phone and desktop widths, in light and dark, and with only the keyboard; that pass found and fixed four bugs.

How it was made

I designed and built it with AI coding tools and reviewed every change. The widget talks to one small interface, so a real business would swap the in-browser demo data for its own API without touching the interface.
